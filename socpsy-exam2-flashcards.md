## F1 · Ch4 glossary (guide scope) — T→D then D→T

**1. [T→D]** self-serving attributional bias
<details><summary>▸</summary>The tendency to attribute failure and other bad events to external circumstances and to attribute success and other good events to oneself.</details>

**12. [T→D]** availability heuristic

**13. [T→D]** representativeness heuristic

## F2 · Ch4 studies, traps, rote

**31. [S→P]** Willis & Todorov (2006): 100 ms vs unlimited time
<details><summary>▸</summary>Snap judgments. Hurried ratings ≈ reflective ratings; impressions form almost instantly.</details>

**32. [S→P]** Todorov et al. (2008): factor analysis of face ratings
<details><summary>▸</summary>Two dimensions: trustworthiness (approach/avoid) and dominance (power, top dog/underdog).</details>

**33. [S→P]** Baby face: round eyes, big forehead, high brows, small chin
<details><summary>▸</summary>Judged weak, naive, submissive (trustworthy + not dominant). Slide label: neoteny. Better treatment as defendants; harder to be seen as fit for "adult" jobs (banking).</details>

**34. [S→P]** Todorov et al. (2005): 1-s faces, congressional races
<details><summary>▸</summary>Faces judged more competent won 69%. Book: predicts CONSENSUS, not proven accuracy.</details>

**35. [S→P]** Ambady & Rosenthal (1993)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Thin slices. Book: three silent 10-s clips of professors → predicted end-of-semester student evaluations. Real "accuracy" still unknown.</details>

**36. [S→P]** Ambady et al. (2002) surgeons [slide-only]
<details><summary>▸</summary>Content-filtered voice. More dominant + less concerned → more likely sued for malpractice.</details>

**37. [S→P]** Antonakis & Dalgas (2009) [slide-only]
<details><summary>▸</summary>Swiss children, "captain of your boat", 2007 French elections → ~72% predicted.</details>

**38. [S→P]** Lau & Russell (1980) sports pages
<details><summary>▸</summary>Self-serving bias: wins → internal (skill, effort); losses → external (refs, weather, luck).</details>

**39. [S→P]** Ross, Amabile & Steinmetz (1977) quiz game
<details><summary>▸</summary>FAE: questioners rated more knowledgeable; their role (writing the questions) was ignored.</details>

**40. [S→P]** Asch (1946) trait list order
<details><summary>▸</summary>Primacy: positive-first list → more favorable. Book: early traits change how later ones are read ("stubborn" = determined vs rigid).</details>

**41. [S→P]** McNeil et al. (1982) physicians
<details><summary>▸</summary>Framing: survival frame 82% chose surgery; mortality frame 56%.</details>

**42. [S→P]** Rasinski (1989) [slide-only]
<details><summary>▸</summary>Framing: "assistance to the poor" ~65% too little vs "welfare" ~20%.</details>

**43. [S→P]** Crocker (1982) tennis workout
<details><summary>▸</summary>Confirmation bias: people check the confirming cell (worked out + won if testing "win").</details>

**44. [S→P]** Snyder & Swann (1978)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Confirmation bias: chose questions that confirm the hypothesis (extravert → "most talkative" type questions). Book follow-up: naive raters judged extravert-tested targets MORE outgoing.</details>

**45. [S→P]** Lord, Ross & Lepper (1979)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Book: MOTIVATED confirmation bias — each side saw the evidence as supporting its own view. Slide: positions held more strongly.</details>

**46. [S→P]** Cohen (1981) librarian/waitress
<details><summary>▸</summary>BOOK = MEMORY: schema-consistent info recalled more accurately (wine/history book vs beer/romance novel). Slide = personality ratings.</details>

**47. [S→P]** Higgins, Rholes & Jones (1977) Donald
<details><summary>▸</summary>Priming: "adventurous, self-confident, independent, persistent" → Donald liked more vs "reckless, conceited, aloof, stubborn". Two "unrelated" experiments.</details>

**48. [S→P]** Denes-Raj & Epstein (1994) bowls
<details><summary>▸</summary>Intuitive vs rational system: 61% chose large bowl (9%) over small (10%).</details>

**49. [S→P]** Slovic et al. (1982) causes of death
<details><summary>▸</summary>Availability: vivid causes (homicide, tornadoes) overestimated vs diabetes, asthma.</details>

**50. [S→P]** Tyler & Cook (1984)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Crime media raises perceived risk to SOCIETY, little effect on personal risk.</details>

**51. [S→P]** Kahneman & Tversky (1973) Tom W.
<details><summary>▸</summary>Representativeness → base-rate neglect: likelihood ranks ≈ similarity ranks, base rates ignored.</details>

**52. [TRAP]** Primacy vs recency: which one is "first info shapes how later info is read"?
<details><summary>▸</summary>Primacy (most often when info is ambiguous). Recency = last items more readily come to mind.</details>

**53. [TRAP]** Crocker: testing "workout → LOSE", which cell do people check most?
<details><summary>▸</summary>Worked out + lost. Direction follows the hypothesis.</details>

**54. [TRAP]** Availability vs representativeness
<details><summary>▸</summary>Availability = ease of recall (how many come to mind). Representativeness = similarity to a prototype.</details>

**55. [TRAP]** FAE vs self-serving bias
<details><summary>▸</summary>FAE = judging OTHERS (traits over situation). Self-serving = judging YOURSELF (success = me, failure = situation).</details>

**56. [TRAP]** Tornado question: slide vs book
<details><summary>▸</summary>Slide: Texas or Kansas. Book: Nebraska or Kansas (same number; people pick Kansas).</details>

**57. [TRAP]** "System 1 / System 2"
<details><summary>▸</summary>Book never uses it. Book: intuitive system / rational system.</details>

**58. [ROTE]** Violent share of crime
<details><summary>▸</summary>Slide: ~17.6% (FBI 2025). Book: ~20% real vs ~80% in media.</details>

**59. [ROTE]** Marble prize
<details><summary>▸</summary>Slide: $1000. Book: $10.</details>

## F4 · Ch5 studies, traps, rote

**86. [S→P]** Emotion vs mood
<details><summary>▸</summary>Emotion: seconds–minutes, specific. Mood: hours–days, unfocused. (Book: disorders = weeks–years.)</details>

**87. [S→P]** Ekman et al. (1969)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Six expressions recognized across U.S., Chile, Brazil, Argentina, Japan. Critique: all saw Western media.</details>

**88. [S→P]** Ekman & Friesen (1971) Fore
<details><summary>▸</summary>Isolated Papua New Guinea group (no movies/magazines). Heard a story → picked the matching face. Universality.</details>

**89. [S→P]** Embarrassment display
<details><summary>▸</summary>Gaze down, self-conscious smile, head down + to side, face touch. Like appeasement in other species; a nonverbal apology.</details>

**90. [S→P]** Dijk, de Jong & Peters (2009) blush
<details><summary>▸</summary>Blushing person after a mishap rated more favorably → remedial function (signals regret).</details>

**91. [S→P]** Tracy & Matsumoto (2008)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Sighted + congenitally blind judo athletes (37 countries): win → pride (head back, chest out, arms up); loss → shame. Innate.</details>

**92. [S→P]** Focal emotions (Mesquita)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Anger focal in honor cultures. Shame/embarrassment focal in interdependent cultures (China: 113+ words vs ~25 English).</details>

**93. [S→P]** Tsai affect valuation
<details><summary>▸</summary>U.S. values excitement (high-arousal positive). East Asia values calm (low-arousal positive).</details>

**94. [S→P]** Muybridge
<details><summary>▸</summary>Stagecoach accident → orbitofrontal cortex damage → impulsive, socially inappropriate, "commitment problem" → killed Harry Larkyns (1874).</details>

**95. [S→P]** Oxytocin
<details><summary>▸</summary>Made in hypothalamus. Builds commitment and trust. Nasal spray → more generous, cooperative, empathic. Slide: Ditzen 2009 couples → more positive talk, lower cortisol.</details>

**96. [S→P]** Niedenthal & Setterlund (1994)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Happy music → faster at happy words; sad music → faster at sad words. Emotions shape perception.</details>

**97. [S→P]** Lerner et al. (2003) after 9/11
<details><summary>▸</summary>Fear (vs anger) → future attacks and own risk judged more likely.</details>

**98. [S→P]** Cultural meaning of happiness
<details><summary>▸</summary>Greece: virtue. Middle Ages: afterlife. Americans: personal achievement. East Asians: harmony + duties. Latino: simpatia.</details>

**99. [S→P]** Two components of happiness
<details><summary>▸</summary>Life satisfaction (evaluation) + emotional well-being (moment-to-moment positive vs negative).</details>

**100. [S→P]** Kahneman et al. (2004)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Happiest: socializing, romantic partners, relaxing. Least: commuting, working, computer.</details>

**101. [S→P]** Gilbert et al. (1998) luckies
<details><summary>▸</summary>Luckies predicted being much unhappier after a breakup than actual leftovers were. Immune neglect.</details>

**102. [S→P]** Fredrickson & Kahneman (1993) film clips
<details><summary>▸</summary>Peak + end predict remembered pleasure; length barely matters (duration neglect).</details>

**103. [S→P]** Kahneman et al. (1993) cold water [slide]
<details><summary>▸</summary>69% chose to repeat the LONGER trial that ended less painfully.</details>

**104. [S→P]** Dunn, Aknin & Norton (2008)
<details><summary>▸</summary>$5 or $20; spending on others → happier than on self. Replicated in 136 cultures.</details>

**105. [S→P]** Lyubomirsky, King & Diener (2005)
<details><summary>▸</summary>225 studies: happiness CAUSES success (relationships, work, health).</details>

**106. [TRAP]** Immune neglect vs focalism
<details><summary>▸</summary>Immune neglect = ignore your own resilience/coping. Focalism = ignore the OTHER events in life.</details>

**107. [TRAP]** Reappraisal vs acceptance
<details><summary>▸</summary>Reappraisal = reinterpret the cause/meaning. Acceptance = open, welcoming attitude to the emotion as is.</details>

**108. [TRAP]** Focal emotion — don't read the morpheme
<details><summary>▸</summary>"Focal" ≠ focus/peak. It means common in a culture.</details>

**109. [TRAP]** "Peak-end rule"
<details><summary>▸</summary>Slide/guide term only. Book term: duration neglect (peak + end matter).</details>

**110. [ROTE]** Happiness low point by age
<details><summary>▸</summary>Book: ~50, rises until ~75. Slide table: 36–50 lowest.</details>

**111. [ROTE]** $75,000
<details><summary>▸</summary>Above it, more money stops raising EMOTIONAL WELL-BEING; life satisfaction still rises (Kahneman & Deaton 2010).</details>

**112. [ROTE]** Happiest countries
<details><summary>▸</summary>Strong safety nets: Sweden, Norway, Finland (World Happiness Report).</details>

**113. [ROTE]** Six universal expressions
<details><summary>▸</summary>Happiness, surprise, sadness, anger, disgust, fear.</details>


## F5 · Ch6 glossary — T→D then D→T

**114. [T→D]** attitude
<details><summary>▸</summary>An evaluation of an object in a positive or negative fashion that includes three components: affect, cognition, and behavior.</details>

**115. [T→D]** Likert scale
<details><summary>▸</summary>A numerical scale used to assess people's attitudes; a scale that includes a set of possible answers with labeled anchors on each extreme.</details>

**116. [T→D]** response latency
<details><summary>▸</summary>The amount of time it takes to respond to a stimulus, such as an attitude question.</details>

<details><summary>▸</summary>An indirect measure of attitudes that doesn't involve a self-report.</details>

**118. [T→D]** cognitive dissonance theory
<details><summary>▸</summary>The theory that inconsistency between a person's thoughts, sentiments, and actions creates an aversive emotional state (dissonance) that leads to efforts to restore consistency.</details>

**119. [T→D]** effort justification
<details><summary>▸</summary>The tendency to reduce dissonance by justifying the time, effort, or money devoted to something that turned out to be unpleasant or disappointing.</details>

**120. [T→D]** induced (forced) compliance
<details><summary>▸</summary>Subtly compelling people to behave in a manner that is inconsistent with their beliefs, attitudes, or values in order to elicit dissonance and therefore a change in their original attitudes and values.</details>

**121. [T→D]** self-affirmation theory
<details><summary>▸</summary>The idea that people can maintain an overall sense of self-worth after being exposed to psychologically threatening information by affirming a valued aspect of themselves unrelated to the threat.</details>

**122. [T→D]** system justification theory
<details><summary>▸</summary>The theory that people are motivated to see the existing sociopolitical system as desirable, fair, and legitimate.</details>

**123. [T→D]** terror management theory (TMT)
<details><summary>▸</summary>The theory that people deal with the potentially crippling anxiety associated with the inevitability of death by striving for symbolic immortality through preserving valued cultural worldviews and by believing they have lived up to their culture's standards.</details>

## F6 · Ch6 studies, traps

**134. [S→P]** Attitude strength measures
<details><summary>▸</summary>Response latency → accessibility (fast = strong). Attitude linkage → centrality (correlated with other attitudes). Implicit measures (affective priming, IAT) for unwilling/unable reporters.</details>

**135. [S→P]** LaPiere (1934)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Refused at 1 of 250+ places; >90% of mailed replies said "no". Book: norms (not making a scene) beat attitudes.</details>

**136. [S→P]** Why attitudes fail to predict behavior (guide "reasons")
<details><summary>▸</summary>1) Conflict with other determinants (norms, conflicting attitudes). 2) Introspecting about reasons misleads. 3) General attitude vs specific behavior mismatch.</details>

**137. [S→P]** Wilson et al. (1984) dating
<details><summary>▸</summary>Listing reasons first → attitude predicted relationship status 9 months later WORSE.</details>

**138. [S→P]** Festinger & Carlsmith (1959)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Boring pegs, told next person "fun". $1 > $20 in rated enjoyment. Insufficient justification → internal change.</details>

**139. [S→P]** Brehm (1956) / free-choice paradigm
<details><summary>▸</summary>After a hard choice: new attractions in the chosen, new flaws in the rejected.</details>

**140. [S→P]** Knox & Inkster (1968) [slide]
<details><summary>▸</summary>Bettors more confident in their horse AFTER betting.</details>

**141. [S→P]** Aronson & Mills (1959)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Severe initiation → boring invertebrate sex talk rated most interesting. Effort justification.</details>

**142. [S→P]** Aronson & Carlsmith (1963) forbidden toy
<details><summary>▸</summary>Mild threat → 36% devalued toy; severe threat → <1%. Insufficient justification.</details>

**143. [S→P]** Linder, Cooper & Jones (1967)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Free choice emphasized → $0.50 group favored the ban more than $2.50. No choice → little change.</details>

**144. [S→P]** Cooper & Worchel (1970)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Low pay → better task rating ONLY when the confederate seemed convinced (negative consequence).</details>

**145. [S→P]** Goethals, Cooper & Naficy (1979)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Essay → committee: change only if consequence foreseen/foreseeable beforehand.</details>

**146. [S→P]** Heine & Lehman (1997)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Japanese: no post-decision dissonance in either condition; unaffected by self-affirmation. Canadians: yes, removed by affirmation.</details>

**147. [S→P]** Kitayama et al. (2004) poster
<details><summary>▸</summary>Faces poster primes "social others" → Japanese show dissonance.</details>

**148. [S→P]** Sakai (1981)
<details><summary>▸</summary>Japanese show induced-compliance dissonance when they think others are watching.</details>

**149. [S→P]** System justification evidence
<details><summary>▸</summary>Women say they deserve less pay; low-income people back the status quo; compensatory stereotypes ("poor but happy"). "Protest is hard; justification is easy."</details>

**150. [S→P]** TMT routes to symbolic immortality
<details><summary>▸</summary>Offspring (men wanting more kids), cultural worldviews/institutions, self-esteem (good standing). Mortality salience → defend worldview.</details>

**151. [S→P]** Landau et al. (2004) [slide]
<details><summary>▸</summary>Mortality salience + 9/11 → more support for Bush, weakest in Democrats.</details>
